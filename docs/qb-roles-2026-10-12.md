# Backup quarterbacks and injured players in weekly projections (Oct 12, 2026)

Reported by Alan: on the Week 6 board, Caleb Williams (CHI, injured) was projected 19.2 and Case Keenum (CHI, QB3) 19.1.

## Cause

AM's projections are **conditional**: `ros_model.py` builds a per-game stat line from the games a player *played*
(a quarterback's per start), and `weekOutlook` showed that number as everyone's weekly projection. Nothing between the
two asked whether he would play:

1. **No depth chart in the projection.** Keenum's line came from his Week 3 start (34 attempts). Every quarterback who
   has started a game was projected as a starter, whatever his place on the depth chart.
2. **No injury status until that week's report.** The only "Out" rule read the current week's injury report, which
   arrives Wednesday; Monday to Wednesday every injured player (Caleb, Lamar, Mayfield, A.J. Brown on IR) kept a full projection.
3. **Relief appearances diluted backups' per-start volume, and the no-prior shrink inflated deep RB / WR / TE backups.**
   A 14%-snap relief game counted as a full start with 9 attempts, while a player with no usable previous season was
   pulled one game toward a *regular starter's* workload (Cyrus Allen, 4% of the snaps: 4.3 PPG from 0.0).

Not a team-level projection copied to several players: each quarterback had his own line, which is why Caleb (19.2) and
Keenum (19.1) were close but not equal. The problem was systemic: in Week 6, **5 teams had two quarterbacks projected
at 10+ points** (CHI, SEA, BAL, TB, WAS) and 41 injured RB / WR / TE kept full projections.

## Correction

A role layer between the projection and the weekly number (`pipeline/roles.py` → `data/roles.json`, refreshed with
every stats run after the depth charts; `roleFor` in the page):

    expected weekly points = conditional projection × chance he plays his role

- **Quarterbacks share one job.** The chance to start cascades down Sleeper's depth chart: P(QB1) = a1,
  P(QB2) = (1 − a1)·a2, P(QB3) = (1 − a1)(1 − a2)·a3. QB3 only starts when both ahead of him are out. QB2 gets a 4%
  relief share; a QB missing from his team's chart gets 0.
- **Missing, unreadable, incomplete or stale roles data (safeguard added at approval).** The page never falls back to
  unadjusted projections for quarterbacks. Malformed entries are dropped; a QB whose team has no quarterbacks in the file
  falls back to his team's starter in its latest game (most snaps; no stats: its highest-ranked QB on the board) at full,
  every other QB of that team 0. A file older than 9 days, or one describing weeks already being played, is still used
  (its depth order still protects backups) but flagged. Each case shows a visible warning (`rolesNote()`): the Week view
  count line, the Matchups source line, the projection's inputs and the console. Checked in `tests/qb_role_check.py`
  (file missing, unreadable, malformed entries: no QB room in the league with two projections of 5+ points; stale file).
- **RB / WR / TE share the field**: their availability alone.
- **Availability** once that game's report is out: none 1.0, Questionable 0.75 (Friday FP 0.9 / LP 0.75 / DNP 0.4),
  Doubtful 0.15, Out / IR 0. Before it (Monday, Tuesday) Sleeper still shows last week's game status, so it only lowers
  the chance (Questionable 0.85, Doubtful 0.5, Out 0.3, IR 0): the starting job is shown as uncertain, not decided.
- **Injured established starters.** Sleeper drops a hurt QB down the chart (CHI: Bagent, Keenum, Williams-Out). A
  short-term-injured QB with more starts (this season + half of last) than the listed QB1 goes back on top and his
  availability decides. A healthy QB the team moved ahead of him keeps the job; IR is never restored.
- **Conditional vs expected.** The weekly number is the expectation; the conditional one stays in the tooltip ("QB3 on
  the depth chart · not expected to start · if he starts: 19.1"). ROS PPG keeps its conditional meaning: a QB whose
  later-week chance to start is under 50% reads "If he starts" and has no QB ROS rank.
- **Model** (`ros_model.py`): a QB's relief appearance (under half the snaps, not injury-shortened) is that share of a
  game of evidence; an RB / WR / TE without a usable previous season is shrunk toward the regulars' workload × his own
  snap share. Backtest (2024–25, 2,108 player-cases): ROS MAE 3.325 → 3.278, rank correlation 0.563 → 0.570.

## Knock-on effects

- **AM Board:** no rank, tier or value changes. The roles layer reads no ranking data and writes none.
- **League-adjusted values:** the next weekly run changes the projection columns of 29 board players (backups); with
  those columns, league-adjusted values were compared in six formats (AM default, 1QB PPR, 10-team 1QB half, 2QB,
  8-team SF, 14-team SF): 0 of 259 values changed.
- **Weekly leaderboards (Week view), Matchups' Live proj, My Team, Waiver Wire, Rosters, the This week card:** show
  expected points, so backups and injured players drop (above), and a lineup that starts a Questionable player projects
  a little lower.
- **ROS PPG:** starters unchanged; backup QBs' conditional number rises (it's now per start, labelled "If he starts");
  deep RB / WR / TE backups without a previous season fall.
- **Stored forecasts:** `data/projections/2026/wk06.json` (stored before this change) is untouched. From Week 7 on each
  stored forecast also keeps `role`, `start`, `depth`, `status` and `n1_exp`, so the expected forecast can be scored as
  it was known before the games; `eval_projections.py` scores n1 and n1_exp against everyone's points (0 when he didn't play).

## Limits

- Depth order and designations come from Sleeper's player database (keyless, refreshed with every stats run). No
  keyless source publishes confirmed starters, so a starting announcement shows up once Sleeper's depth chart reflects it.
- The availability numbers are fixed priors, not fitted; the stored forecasts will show how well they hold.
- Later-week roles don't model new injuries or how long a short-term injury lasts.
